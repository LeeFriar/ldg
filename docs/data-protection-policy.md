# LDG Electrical data-protection policy

Owner: Lloyd Garland, sole trader  
Last reviewed: 20 August 2026  
Review: annually and whenever processing changes

This internal policy supports the public Privacy Notice and the ICO’s recommendations for sole traders. It is an operational document and is not published on the website.

## Responsibilities

Lloyd Garland is responsible for personal information handled by LDG Electrical. This includes identifying why information is needed, limiting what is collected, keeping it accurate and secure, responding to rights requests, applying the retention schedule and reviewing suppliers.

## Working rules

- Collect only information needed to respond to enquiries, quote, carry out work, certify work, invoice and comply with legal obligations.
- Explain how information is used by linking to the website Privacy Notice when details are first collected, including in email signatures and quotations.
- Do not use customer information for unrelated marketing without an appropriate lawful basis.
- Keep business email and devices protected with strong unique passwords, multi-factor authentication where available, current security updates and device locking.
- Do not leave customer paperwork visible or unattended. Store it securely and destroy it when the retention period ends.
- Verify recipients before sending personal information. Use blind copy where an email is sent to unrelated recipients.
- Give access only to service providers or people who need information for the work, and share only the minimum necessary.
- Review retained email, job and finance records at least annually against the retention schedule.

## Individual rights requests

Record the date, identity, scope and outcome of any request. Verify identity proportionately, search relevant email, job and finance records, and respond without undue delay and normally within one month. Do not erase information that must lawfully be retained; explain any applicable exemption or restriction.

## Personal data breaches

A breach includes accidental loss, destruction, alteration, unauthorised access or disclosure. On discovery:

1. Contain the incident and protect accounts or devices.
2. Record what happened, the information and people affected, likely consequences and remedial action.
3. Assess risk to the affected people.
4. Notify the ICO without undue delay and, where required, within 72 hours of awareness unless the breach is unlikely to risk people’s rights and freedoms.
5. Inform affected people without undue delay where the breach is likely to create a high risk.
6. Preserve the incident record even when notification is not required.

## Supplier and processing changes

Before adopting a new email, cloud, payment, analytics or customer-management service, check what data it receives, where it is processed, its security, retention and contract terms. Update the Privacy Notice before beginning materially different processing. Non-essential website storage or tracking must not be enabled without reviewing PECR consent requirements.

## ICO fee

Use the ICO registration self-assessment and review the result if business processing changes. Keep evidence of the assessment or registration.
